**Text (Secular Version):** (delete that header, it is unnecessary or we find a better term)

Today let us pause and be genuinely thankful. We have come to gentler pathways and smoother roads in our mind training. Even if a bit of wavering remains—some small objections or a quiet hesitance—the stubborn resistance to truth has softened, and you can be deeply grateful for gains that are far greater than you yet realize.

In a secular and Jungian light, giving thanks to our "Father" means honoring the Unconditioned Source of life—the Dao, the loving ground of being—that has never abandoned us to wander in the dark alone. As Kenneth Wapnick notes, our deepest gratitude is for being saved from the small, fearful ego-self we thought we made. Our true worth far transcends our petty self-judgments and meager comparisons; beneath every passing doubt, our shared Identity remains changeless, whole, and held in unbroken love.

Today in gratitude we lift our hearts above despair and raise our eyes, no longer looking downward to the dust. As Sean Reagan reflects, when we remember that a quiet inner Friend—the Higher Self—walks beside us, our steps naturally lighten. And as we offer our appreciation outward, smiling on everyone we meet, that gratitude is multiplied a thousandfold, filling our awareness with gladness, strength, and peace.

**ACIM & Science:**

*Neurobiology of Gratitude (Dr. Robert Emmons):* Regular gratitude practice stimulates hypothalamic regulation, boosts dopamine and serotonin pathways, and shifts cognitive appraisal from scarcity to abundance.

*Broaden-and-Build Theory of Positive Emotions (Dr. Barbara Fredrickson):* Experiencing heartfelt appreciation expands perceptual awareness, dissolves tunnel-vision threat focus, and builds lasting psychological resilience.

*Prosocial Co-Regulation & Warmth (Dacher Keltner):* Expressing genuine warmth—even through a gentle smile and unhurried presence—triggers vagal and oxytocin pathways that calm both giver and receiver.
