## 1. Dimension Definitions

| Dimension | Definition | Scoring anchor |
|---|---|---|
| **Groundedness (G)** | Every claim traceable to repo artifacts; zero invented statistics or capabilities | 10 = fully sourced; ≤4 = fabricated claims detected |
| **Clarity (C)** | Structural legibility: headline → hierarchy → proof, readable by a non-specialist decision-maker | 10 = deployable copy as-is |
| **Actionability (A)** | Can the portfolio execute the recommendation this week with existing resources? | 10 = same-week executable |
| **Compliance (K)** | Safety architecture, consent protocols, red lines, honest-claims discipline | 10 = explicit red lines + safety scaling rules |
| **Commercial Viability (V)** | Revenue logic: pricing, channel, measurable partner value | 10 = complete monetization system |

Scores are rolling averages across rounds (max 10.0). Head-to-head wins count regardless of margin.

## 4. Operational Strengths & Weaknesses per Profile

### `marketing-executive`
**Strengths**
- Fastest path from artifact to deployable positioning; copy arrives launch-ready.
- Consistent grounding discipline — all hooks anchored in real portfolio content across both rounds.
- Strong packaging instinct: converts constraints into features ("phones stay in baskets" as digital-detox hook; retention metrics as partner deliverables).
- Channel reasoning includes negative selection (what NOT to buy).

**Weaknesses**
- Weakest on safety/compliance depth: defers to existing protocols rather than extending them to new formats (flagged in R2).
- Outcome overpromising risk at compressed price points (Tier 1 expectation gap).
- Rejection-framed headlines can alienate adjacent audiences (R1 self-declared, adjudicated).
- Underestimates staffing/operational cost of scaled formats.

**Deployment guidance**: acquisition, pricing, partner-facing materials, go-to-market. Pair with a compliance-bearing profile for anything touching participant formats.

### `workshop-designer`
**Strengths**
- Only profile recording a perfect dimension score (Compliance 10.0): consent architecture, ratios, staffing-scaling rules always explicit.
- Correctly identifies the causal chain from experiential quality to commercial outcome (retention via landing sequence).
- Red lines are concrete and operational, not rhetorical.
- Designs monetization perimeters that respect state work (seams, not cores).

**Weaknesses**
- Leaves near-term revenue on the table; perimeter-only models miss festival/pipeline opportunities.
- Limited external narrative instinct — brand coherence implicit rather than packaged.
- Single-round sample; scores may regress once judged on pure commercial briefs.

**Deployment guidance**: any participant-facing format design, safety annexes, quality gates on marketing claims about experiences. Mandatory reviewer whenever a commercial tier compresses an experiential arc.

### `research-strategist`
**Strengths**
- Best frame-generator in the program: produced the strategically superior positioning (hypothesis-testing > dogma-negation), adopted into the messaging ladder despite losing the round.
- Identifies structural category escapes rather than incremental differentiation.
- Strong trust-signal awareness (safety architecture positioned as differentiator).
- Honest self-risk declaration — surfaced its own audience-fit weakness unprompted.

**Weaknesses**
- Lowest actionability on record (6.0): recommendations depend on unbuilt infrastructure (referral networks, CE partnerships, sources layer).
- "Evidence-informed" language currently exceeds the portfolio's citation base — groundedness liability until `/ACIM/sources/` is hardened.
- Audience drift toward professional gatekeepers (therapists) away from the brief's broader seeker segment.
- Slow channels; no named partners or pilots anywhere in submission.

**Deployment guidance**: upstream strategy, message architecture, objection forecasting, competitive framing. Do not assign launch-critical deliverables until paired with an executor profile.
