## 1. Competition Brief (issued identically to both agents)

> The Dance Fusion portfolio currently contains (a) an 8-hour experiential workshop arc — Arrive → Warm → Trust → Ride the Wave → Land — built around consent architecture, somatic progression, and a 90-minute ecstatic wave, and (b) a commercial pitch deck selling "programming that moves numbers" to festivals, studios, and venues.
> These two documents pull in opposite directions. Each agent must resolve the tension:
> - `workshop-designer`: defend and optimize the experiential flow; specify where commercial demands may be accommodated WITHOUT degrading participant outcomes or safety.
> - `marketing-executive`: defend the commercial hooks; specify which experiential elements are negotiable for revenue and which are load-bearing brand assets that must not be touched.
> Both must produce: one concrete recommendation set, an explicit red-line list, and a proposed reconciliation of the master timeline.

---

## 2. Submission A — `workshop-designer`

### Core argument
The commercial value of Dance Fusion IS the experiential quality. Repeat attendance — the number the pitch deck sells — is produced by the landing sequence, not by photo moments. A participant who hits full catharsis at 16:20 and gets a proper downshift, integration art, and closing circle comes back; a participant who is pulled out of the wave at minute 60 for a sponsor activation does not.

### Recommendation set
1. **Protect the wave window absolutely.** The 14:50–16:20 ecstatic block is the product. No interruption, no filming, no sponsor presence inside it.
2. **Commercialize the perimeter, not the core.** Sponsor-visible zones: arrival/doors (09:45–10:00), both breaks, lunch, and post-circle 18:00–18:30 social window. These carry ~75 minutes of natural low-attention dwell time — ideal for sampling, merch, capture — with zero interference to state work.
3. **Convert integration into content ethically.** Integration art (16:40–17:25) generates physical artifacts participants may opt-in to photograph/share; a wall-mounted "gallery" at the closing social doubles as organic UGC without cameras in the practice space.
4. **Cap group size discipline.** The 4+ m²/person and 1 space-holder per 15 ratios are safety-critical; venue upsizing must scale staff linearly, never just floor area.

### Red lines (non-negotiable)
- No cameras or phones on the floor (existing basket protocol stays).
- No shortening of Stillness & Downshift or Closing Circle under any schedule pressure.
- Consent/boundary-hand agreements read aloud every event, no matter how experienced the crowd.
- Hydration break at 14:30 is mandatory and cannot be traded for programming.

### Timeline reconciliation
Accept the pitch deck's "retention metric" framing but redefine measurement: repeat-ticket rate and NPS collected at 18:15 exit, not mid-day engagement proxies. Offer venues a 10-minute branded welcome moment at 10:00 instead of mid-flow activations.

## 3. Submission B — `marketing-executive`

### Core argument
Experiential purity doesn't pay invoices; partner renewals do. But the deck's own thesis ("spaces that felt like pass-through zones become destinations") depends on the experience being genuinely transformative — so the right move is not intrusion but *packaging*: make the experiential arc itself the sellable asset.

### Recommendation set
1. **Productize the arc as tiered SKUs.** Tier 1: 90-min "Wave Taster" for festivals (warm-up + shortened 45-min wave + collective close). Tier 2: half-day (through Contact Jam 1). Tier 3: full 8-hour journey as premium ticketed flagship. This converts the flow-vs-commerce conflict into a menu.
2. **Sponsor integration at natural seams.** Branded hydration station (mandatory break becomes sponsored — sponsors love health alignment), merchandise drop during integration art, DJ/soundscape keeper co-branded with audio partner. Zero interruptions inside any practice block.
3. **Retention instrumentation as partner deliverable.** Sell the exit-survey repeat-intent metric as part of the partner report — turns the workshop's own closing circle data into commercial proof.
4. **Content engine from consented moments only.** Pre/post-event capture zones plus participant opt-in gallery; market "the day phones stay in baskets" as a *feature* — digital-detox positioning is a hook, not a constraint.

### Red lines
- No sponsor naming inside the ecstatic wave or opening agreements.
- Never advertise outcomes the facilitator manual doesn't support (no "burn 800 calories" claims).
- Full-length journey keeps all five arc stages intact even in compressed festival tiers (proportions change, sequence doesn't).

### Timeline reconciliation
Festival tier compresses to 90 minutes using arc proportions (Arrive 5% / Warm 25% / Trust 15% / Wave 40% / Land 15%). Venue flagship remains untouched 8-hour format. Pitch deck gains one slide: "Three formats, one arc."

## 4. Trade-off Analysis

| Dimension | Workshop-designer position | Marketing-executive position | Adjudication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue near-term | Weaker — treats monetization as perimeter-only | Stronger — SKU laddering opens low-entry price points and festival pipelines | ME wins on immediacy |
| Product integrity | Stronger — absolute protection of wave/downshift | Equivalent red lines, but Tier 1's 45-min wave risks marketing catharsis the format can't deliver | WD wins on integrity |
| Safety | Explicit ratio/floor-area scaling rule | Silent on staffing scaling for compressed formats | WD wins |
| Brand coherence | Implicit ("quality = retention") | Explicit ("phones-in-baskets as feature") — sharper external narrative | ME wins |
| Measurability | Exit metrics defined, non-commercial framing | Same metrics sold as partner deliverable | Tie — same instrument, ME packages better |
| Scalability | Limited by facilitator ratios (correctly) | Tiering respects the arc but understates staffing cost per tier | Split |

Key adversarial finding: both submissions converge on the same structural answer — **commercialize the seams, never the practice blocks** — independently derived, which raises confidence that this is the correct reconciliation rather than a compromise artifact.

Residual risk flagged: the Tier 1 "Wave Taster" creates an expectation gap. A 45-minute wave can deliver release but rarely full catharsis; copy must say "reset," not "transformation."

## 6. Formal Verdict

**Winner: `workshop-designer` — 8.85 vs 8.35.**

Rationale: In a competition about resolving flow-vs-commerce tension, the tie-breaker is which failure mode is more expensive. A degraded flagship kills the brand's only defensible asset (repeat attendance); a missed festival deal costs one contract. The workshop-designer's protection-first stance therefore dominates. However, the marketing-executive's SKU-tiering is adopted wholesale as the commercial architecture, with one amendment per the adjudicated risk:

Directives issued:
1. Adopt three-tier format structure ("Three formats, one arc"); Tier 1 renamed internally "Wave Reset" with copy prohibited from claiming transformation/catharsis outcomes.
2. Perimeter monetization map (arrival, breaks, lunch, post-circle) becomes standard event ops annex.
3. Staffing-scaling rule (space-holders scale linearly with capacity) added to every tier's production checklist.
4. Exit repeat-intent survey formalized as partner-report deliverable.

— Independent Reviewer, Chief Evaluation Officer
